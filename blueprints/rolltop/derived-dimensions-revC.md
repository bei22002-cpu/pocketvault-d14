## Findings that change the design

- **The loaded pouch sinks.** It displaces 389 g of seawater and weighs 465 g with a device in it, so net buoyancy is -76 g. The wrist tether is not an accessory, it is the retention system. A float collar in the D16 pattern is the alternative.
- **The bezel sets the cavity, not the phone.** The weld flange is 196 mm tall, which forces a 212 mm cavity for a 165 mm device — 48 mm of depth that carries no payload. This is the direct cost of a display-sized rigid window.
- **Glass dominates the mass budget** at 96 g of 225 g. Dropping the pane to 2.9 mm, the calculated minimum, would save about a fifth of the total, at the cost of impact margin on a boat deck.
- **Clamp threshold is derived, not chosen.** 72 N per cell comes from holding 3x internal pressure across the roll contact patch. It still has to survive a dunk test before it goes in firmware.

## Still open

- The bezel is PPA GF30 and the body is TPU-faced aramid. Those do not RF-weld to each other, which is why the bezel carries an overmoulded TPU weld lip. Weld trials are unproven.
- Every number above is geometry and hand calculation. Nothing has been submerged. The depth rating is a design target until the dunk and proof tests are run.
- Capacitive touch through a 3.0 mm glass pane with a 1.5 mm air gap does not work. Rev C is
  view-only through the window; all input has to come from elsewhere.
