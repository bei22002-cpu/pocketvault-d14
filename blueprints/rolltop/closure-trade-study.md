# D14 closure trade study — is the roll-top right at 10 m?

Generated by `closure_trade.py`. Rated depth 10 m, internal pressure 101 kPa, mouth width 119 mm.

> **Acted on.** The recommendation below is built as Rev D, drawing `D14-RT-004`. Rev D moved the latches inboard of the mouth ends rather than onto them, which cut the span and took the bar from the 8.0 mm assumed here down to 6.0 mm.

## Seal load: the bar asks for less

Both closures have to hold 3x internal pressure across their contact strip, so the load follows the strip width. A 6 mm bar face needs 1.81 N/mm, or 215 N across the mouth, against the 287 N the roll needs over its wider 8 mm bundle — 25% less clamp for the user to generate. A narrow, well-defined seal line is cheaper to load than a wide, vague one.

## What sizes a clamped bar

A bar seals only if it stays flat against the gasket between its latches. With a 1.5 mm bead compressed 30%, the squeeze is 0.45 mm, and spending more than 25% of that on mid-span bow means the centre of the mouth is sealing on less than the ends. That caps bow at 0.112 mm and is the binding constraint.

Deflection governs everywhere — peak stress never exceeds 38 MPa against flexural strengths in the hundreds, so these bars are stiffness-limited, not strength-limited. Three consequences:

- **Material barely matters.** Every two-latch bar lands between 31 and 32 g whatever it is made of, because stiffness goes as thickness cubed while mass goes as thickness times density, and the two effects very nearly cancel across this material set. Choosing aluminium buys a thinner bar, not a lighter one.
- **Two latches does not work in plastic.** A PPA bar latched only at its ends needs to be 20 mm thick, which is thicker than the whole 18.5 mm pouch. Span enters as the fourth power, so this is not a small miss.
- **Either add a third latch or go metal.** A centre latch halves the span and drops the PPA bar to 8.0 mm and 13 g. Keeping two latches and switching to aluminium gives 10.9 mm and 31 g.

## The reason to switch is not mass

Mass is a wash. The case for the bar is that it changes the character of the leak path and the honesty of the sensing:

- **Leak path.** A roll seals along a spiral, and the ends of that spiral are where dry bags actually leak: the folded edges form a small channel that no amount of strap tension closes. A bar seals along a straight line against a defined gasket, which is a geometry you can inspect, measure and calculate.
- **Creep.** Roll contact pressure is carried by TPU-faced fabric in sustained compression, which relaxes. A 10 m rating held for 30 minutes is exactly the duration over which that relaxation matters, and it is not something the load cells can correct for once the user has walked away from the buckle.
- **Sensing fidelity.** This is the strongest argument. On the roll, the cells read strap tension through a compressible 10 mm bundle, so they infer interface pressure through the most compliant element in the stack. On a bar, the cells sit directly in the load path under the gasket line. The clamp-verification feature the whole product is named for only measures what it claims to measure in the bar version.

## What the bar costs

- A second rigid part, roughly mouth-width, that does not collapse. The `designs.js` warning that the hard bezel edge is an impact weak point now applies to the bar as well.
- Grit tolerance goes down, not up. A roll closes over sand; a gasket does not. For a beach and boat-deck product that is a real regression and argues for a wipeable, exposed gasket land rather than a recessed groove.
- Three latches is three user actions instead of two, and a centre latch is easy to forget.

## Recommendation

Move to a clamped bar with a centre latch, 8.0 mm in PPA GF30, and keep the four load cells but relocate them into the bar and its land. It is mass-neutral, it turns a spiral friction seal into a straight gasket line, and it makes the sensing measure the seal instead of a proxy for it. If the third latch tests badly with cold hands, the fallback is two latches with the 10.9 mm aluminium bar at 31 g.

The rigid gasketed lid is not carried forward. At this mouth width it is a rigid case top, which ends the soft-body premise that puts D14 in the non-rigid family at all. If the programme wants a genuine 10 m gasketed closure, the honest move is to build an A-family rigid case, not to armour a pouch until it becomes one.

## Still unproven

- The 0.112 mm bow budget is an assumption about how unevenly a gasket can be loaded before it leaks. It needs a bench test on a bar with a deliberately sloppy centre latch.
- Nothing here changes the fact that the bezel-to-body weld and the glass bond are still the two longest unvalidated seal paths on the product. The closure is not the only risk, and fixing it does not make the pouch waterproof on its own.
