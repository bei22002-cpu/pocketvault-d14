# D14 Rolltop — Product Proposal Speaker Notes

**Runtime:** 10 minutes presenting + 15–20 minutes for objections and the decision  
**Deck:** `blueprints/d14-product-pitch.html` — `←` `→` navigate · `N` presenter notes · `A` jump to appendix · `F` fullscreen  
**Leave-behind:** `blueprints/d14-product-pitch-brief.html` (print, send 24–48 h ahead as a pre-read)

This is a **product adoption decision**, not a manufacturing budget vote. If the room turns to P1 dollars, switch to `d14-approval.html`.

---

## Why the deck is built this way

The structure follows four evidence-backed practices rather than a generic template.

| Practice | Source | What it changed here |
|---|---|---|
| **BLUF** — lead with the decision request in the first 30 seconds | Executive-communication standard for internal decision meetings | The ask moved from slide 12 to slide 1 |
| **Assertion–evidence slides** — sentence headline stating the takeaway, supported by visual evidence instead of bullet lists | Alley & Garner, Penn State; 110-student study found significantly better comprehension, fewer misconceptions, lower cognitive load, stronger delayed recall (p < .01) | Every headline is now a claim, not a topic label |
| **Strategic narrative** — name the change, name the enemy, tease the promised land, then product, evidence last | Raskin's five-element sales narrative | Slides 3–8 reordered; product no longer opens the deck |
| **Contrast / sparkline** — alternate "what is" and "what could be," and name resistance directly | Duarte | Added the enemy slide, the promised-land slide, and an explicit trade-offs slide |
| **Core deck + appendix** — 10–12 core slides, detail pulled on request | Current pitch-deck convention | Spec, cost bands, and risk register moved to appendix |

The trade-offs slide is the one most proposals omit. Naming what a yes costs — before anyone asks — is what makes the recommendation credible.

## Timed script

### 1 — The ask (30 sec)
No warm-up, no agenda slide. “I'm asking for one decision today: adopt D14 Rolltop as our flagship product, and fund the RFQ plus demo build, so we can open eight to fifteen wholesale doors before December 16.”

**Then pause.** Let them register that this is a decision meeting.

### 2 — Bottom line (45 sec)
The whole proposal on one page: ask, what it buys, the numbers. If someone is ready to approve here, jump to trade-offs (slide 10) and close early.

### 3 — What changed (60 sec)
External shift, not our problem. “Phones went everywhere water is, and protection never followed. Fifty-five percent of the people we surveyed already ruined something. Sixty-two percent carry nothing.”

### 4 — The enemy (60 sec) ★
“Our competitor is not an eighty-dollar dry bag. It's a sandwich bag and ‘I'll be careful.’” This reframes price for the whole rest of the meeting — we are not asking anyone to trade down.

### 5 — Promised land (20 sec)
Say it and move. “A counter display that empties, and a buyer who texts us for more. Not a catalog. Not a survey. Reorders.”

### 6 — The product (60 sec)
Now the product, framed as what gets a store to that outcome. Hold up a sample if you have one.

### 7 — How it works (45 sec)
Three beats, fast. The real point: a clerk can demo this in twenty seconds with no training.

### 8 — Evidence (75 sec)
“We didn't guess the price — the buyers set it.” Then volunteer the weakness before anyone finds it: 34% top-2 intent and an 81%-student sample. Volunteering it is what makes the rest credible.

### 9 — Business model (75 sec)
Stores are the customer. Give the keystone math out loud — buyers think in margin, not features. Opening order is deliberately small: week one buys shelf space, not revenue.

### 10 — Trade-offs (90 sec) ★
Name all three costs plainly: the flagship can't be the $19.99 SKU, cases freeze cash, and this shelves the other two products for the window.

**Rehearse this transition out loud:** “Having said all three of those, I still recommend yes.” That sentence has to carry the weight of having heard the costs.

### 11 — Decision (45 sec)
Read what changes at yes. Read what changes at no. **Then stop talking.** The silence is the close — do not fill it.

## Likely objections

| Objection | Answer |
|-----------|--------|
| “Isn't this just a dry bag?” | Dry bags protect the phone by making it unusable. Survey ranked waterproof #1 and touchscreen as the differentiator. |
| “$19.99 with electronics in it?” | No — and I won't claim it. The store SKU is de-featured. The instrumented unit is the demo. Appendix has the bands. |
| “Your sample is all students.” | Correct, 81%. That's why we start campus-adjacent doors and not a regional chain. |
| “34% intent is weak.” | Agreed, and that's why the model is demo-led wholesale rather than packaging and ads. |
| “Why not sell direct on Instagram?” | Brand proof only. Revenue is store reorders. Stores get MAP protection or they won't stock us. |
| “Is it waterproof?” | The drawing says water-resistant. It gets the waterproof hangtag only after lot dunk-QC passes. |
| “What about the wallet roll-up?” | That was locked as v1 in `DECISION.md`. This proposal asks to change that. One SKU for this window — that's trade-off three. |
| “What does this cost us?” | RFQ plus demo serials now. Production tooling and inventory are a separate, later vote. |
